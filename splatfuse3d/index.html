Overview / Abstract

This thesis investigates geometry-verified supervision for hierarchical object and part segmentation in 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes reconstructed from multi-view RGB images. The proposed approach combines a planar-based Gaussian representation, unbiased depth rendering, surface-normal estimation, and single-view and multi-view geometric consistency with multi-scale 2D segmentation masks. Scale-conditioned affinity features, scale-gated representations, and contrastive learning are used to support segmentation at different physical scales. The central contribution is a geometry-based verification mechanism that reprojects 3D points between camera views using rendered depth and evaluates mask agreement while accounting for visibility and depth consistency. The resulting confidence scores are used to weight or reject unreliable cross-view training pairs. The learned representations are then used to segment individual objects and their constituent parts, with per-object mesh extraction investigated through masked depth fusion. The method will be evaluated against existing segmentation baselines using segmentation accuracy, boundary leakage, cross-view consistency, and mesh-quality metrics, alongside ablation studies to measure the effect of geometry verification.
